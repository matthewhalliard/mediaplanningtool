# Bionic vs Halliard: Which Media Planning Tool Is Right for Your Agency?

If you're evaluating media planning software in 2026, Bionic almost certainly shows up on your list. It's been around long enough to earn serious credibility — strong reviews on G2 and Capterra, a robust content library, and a product genuinely built around how agencies produce plans. For a long time, it was the obvious choice for independent shops that wanted something purpose-built for media.

Halliard is the newer entrant, built by a team with deep roots in media — including stints at Starcom, Dentsu, Amazon DSP, and Paramount. The pitch isn't "Bionic but cheaper." It's a different product philosophy: plan-to-proof in one platform, with capabilities like cross-channel reach & frequency modeling and agentic buying that Bionic doesn't offer at all.

This comparison lays out both tools honestly. If Bionic is the better fit, we'll tell you so.

## Quick Overview

**[Bionic](/bionic)** launched as a media planning-focused SaaS and has methodically built out every stage of the traditional plan production workflow: RFP generation, vendor management, IO creation, flowcharts, trafficking instructions, and reconciliation. It's a mature product with a clear philosophy — make the plan creation process faster and more professional. It backs that up with Bionic Academy certification and a library of 15+ comparison pages that demonstrate how seriously they take the media planning category.

**[Halliard](/halliard)** describes itself as "the OS for Modern Media Teams." The vision is broader: start with a visual timeline planner across 40+ channels, layer in AI-assisted planning, and connect the planning workflow all the way through to buy execution and real-world measurement. Founded by Matthew Jacobs (15+ years across Starcom, Dentsu SVP Product, Amazon DSP Sr. PM, Paramount Sr. Director), Halliard is designed specifically for independent agencies and in-house teams managing $10M–$500M+ in spend.

## Pricing: A Real Difference in Access

This is the comparison that matters most for smaller shops.

**Bionic** is priced at **$199/user/month**, with a hard floor of a 5-user minimum — meaning the entry point is **$995/month** before you've done anything. Annual pre-pay brings that to $175/user/month. For a 10-person team, you're looking at $1,750–$1,990/month. That's not unreasonable for a large agency, but it's a significant line item for an independent shop with 3–5 planners.

**Halliard** offers a **free plan** with no credit card required — individual planners can get in and start building. Premium and Enterprise tiers scale up from there (contact for pricing). The free plan isn't a crippled demo; it's designed to let planners experience the core product.

For a boutique agency or a newly independent planner, that access gap is meaningful. Bionic's minimum means you're committed to nearly $12,000/year before you know if the product fits your workflow.

## Where Halliard Differentiates

### Cross-Channel Reach & Frequency — A Unique Capability

This is the biggest functional gap in the Bionic product, and it's not a minor one. Halliard built a **proprietary 10,000-person panel across 210 DMAs** that enables true cross-channel reach & frequency modeling — television, digital, audio, streaming, all in one calculation. No other tool at the independent agency level offers this. The R+F problem has historically forced agencies to either buy expensive third-party tools, rely on publisher-provided estimates (which are optimistic by nature), or just skip the analysis entirely. Halliard closes that gap natively.

For agencies pitching competitive accounts or defending media mix decisions to clients, this isn't a nice-to-have. It's the kind of capability that used to require holding company infrastructure.

### Agentic Buying: Real Execution, Not a Marketing Claim

Both Bionic and Halliard use the word "agentic" in the context of AI — but they mean very different things.

Bionic's "first agentic AI" positioning refers to planning tools designed to help agencies manage AI-driven ad products like Google's Performance Max and Meta's Advantage+. That's a legitimate use case: structuring inputs, budgets, and creative instructions for algorithmic campaigns.

Halliard executed something categorically different: **the first fully autonomous media transaction via MCP with PubMatic in December 2025**. That's not planning assistance for AI ad products — that's an AI agent placing an actual buy on behalf of a human planner. The distinction matters. One is a better interface for AI products. The other is a step-change in how media is transacted. Halliard's PubMatic MCP integration puts agentic execution within reach for independent agencies today.

### Real-Time Performance Tracking

Bionic's workflow ends at plan production and reconciliation. Halliard integrates directly with **18+ platforms** — Google, Meta, TikTok, Spotify, Netflix, YouTube, Twitch, Hulu, and more — pulling in real-time spend data so planners can see actuals against plan without toggling between dashboards. That closed loop between planning and performance is genuinely different from the Bionic approach.

### Plan-to-Proof Workflow

Halliard's architecture is designed around keeping everything in one place: the visual timeline planner, AI assistant, R+F modeling, scenario planning, MMM, agentic execution, and measurement. Institutional knowledge — every plan, every result, every lesson — stays in the platform rather than living in disconnected spreadsheets, PDFs, and email threads. For agencies building a replicable process, that compounding value is significant.

### Pricing Accessibility

The free plan isn't just a marketing move — it reflects a genuine product philosophy. Halliard is designed for agencies that can't absorb a $1,000/month floor before proving ROI. A solo planner or two-person shop can start using Halliard for free, build workflows, and scale up. That path doesn't exist with Bionic.

## FAQ

### Is Bionic worth $995/month for a small agency?

That depends entirely on team size and workflow. For a 5-person team where plan production is a significant time sink, $199/user/month can generate positive ROI. But for a 2–3 person shop, the minimum seat requirement makes the effective cost much higher per user. Bionic doesn't offer a free plan or a lower entry point, so smaller agencies should weigh that cost against the specific features they'd actually use. Halliard's free tier exists specifically for planners in that situation.

### Does Bionic have cross-channel reach & frequency modeling?

No. Bionic does not offer a cross-channel R+F engine. Its toolset is focused on plan production, IO/RFP management, and trafficking. If cross-channel R+F is a requirement for your planning process or client deliverables, you'd need a separate third-party tool — or Halliard, which has a proprietary 10,000-person panel built in.

### What does "agentic AI" mean in the context of Bionic vs Halliard?

These terms are used differently by each company. Bionic's agentic AI features are tools that help planners work more effectively with AI-driven ad products like Google Performance Max and Meta Advantage+. Halliard's agentic capability refers to executing actual media transactions autonomously via MCP integration with PubMatic — the first such execution in the industry (December 2025). One improves planning inputs for algorithmic products; the other automates the buy itself.

### Can I use Halliard alongside Bionic?

Technically yes, but practically the platforms overlap significantly. Both offer plan creation, AI assistance, and workflow management. The more useful question is whether you're using Bionic primarily for its mature IO/flowchart features and want to supplement with Halliard's R+F engine — which is feasible — or whether you want to consolidate into a single platform. Most agencies evaluating both end up choosing one.

### How does Halliard handle flowcharts compared to Bionic?

Bionic's flowchart generation is one of its strongest features — polished, deeply integrated with the plan data, and a genuine time-saver for agencies that produce a lot of flowcharts for presentations. Halliard includes flowcharting within its visual timeline planner (Gantt-style, 40+ channels), and the functionality is solid. If flowcharts specifically are your primary evaluation criterion, Bionic has more development history in that area. If you need flowcharts as one part of a broader plan-to-proof workflow, Halliard covers it.

### Is Halliard only for large agencies?

No. Halliard's free plan and pricing model are explicitly designed for independent agencies and individual planners — it's one of the core differences from tools like Bionic or Mediaocean that have high minimum spend thresholds. The platform scales up to handle Enterprise needs for larger in-house teams, but the entry point is accessible to a solo planner or boutique shop.
