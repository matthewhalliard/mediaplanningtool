**The definitive truth set on the American consumer — 60,000+ data elements, probabilistic survey methodology, and direct audience activation**

## What Is MRI-Simmons?

MRI-Simmons is a consumer research and intelligence platform that provides US-focused audience data for media planning, brand strategy, and programmatic activation. It is widely regarded as the gold standard for understanding American consumers — covering demographics, psychographics, media consumption, brand preferences, purchase behavior, and lifestyle attitudes at a depth that no other single-source US dataset matches.

The company was formed in 2019 as a joint venture between GfK and SymphonyAI Group, merging two of the most trusted consumer research brands in the United States: MRI (Media Research Inc.), whose Survey of the American Consumer held Media Rating Council accreditation for over 20 years, and Simmons Research, which ran the National Consumer Study continuously since 1962. In 2023, GfK merged with NielsenIQ (NIQ), making NIQ the majority owner of MRI-Simmons today.

This history matters. MRI-Simmons carries over 60 years of methodological rigor, cross-industry credibility, and data continuity — which is why it is embedded in agency workflows across the US as an RFP standard rather than an optional research supplement.

### What MRI-Simmons Is Used For

Planners use MRI-Simmons for four primary purposes:

1. **Consumer profiling** — building vivid, multi-dimensional portraits of target audiences using demographics, psychographics, lifestyle statements, and brand behavior
2. **Media planning** — identifying which media channels, titles, and platforms reach a defined audience most efficiently
3. **Audience activation** — pushing validated audience segments from planning directly into programmatic media buys
4. **Brand strategy** — benchmarking brand perception, tracking competitive share, and understanding which consumer attributes predict brand preference

## The Data: 60,000+ Consumer Elements

MRI-Simmons USA — the flagship study — measures more than 60,000 consumer elements across a nationally representative sample of 50,000+ US adults per year. These data points span:

- **Demographics**: age, income, education, household composition, employment, language, acculturation
- **Psychographics**: 800+ lifestyle statements and 1,000+ attitudes and opinions covering values, motivations, and worldview
- **Brand behavior**: 6,500+ brands tracked across 1,000+ product categories
- **Media consumption**: reach, time spent, and activity across traditional and digital media including TV, streaming, print, radio, social, and apps
- **Purchase intent and spending**: shopping behavior, channel preferences, spending levels by category
- **Multicultural data**: Hispanic acculturation, language preference, country of origin, and culturally specific attitudes
- **Youth studies**: brand preferences, media habits, and lifestyles for kids 6–11, teens 12–19

The 90+ proprietary audience segments built from this data allow planners to identify and target audiences that would be impossible to define from media exposure data alone.

### What Makes MRI-Simmons Data Different

The key methodological differentiator is **address-based probabilistic sampling**. Rather than recruiting from an online opt-in panel, MRI-Simmons contacts respondents at random from physical address records. This means:

- Every US adult has a known, non-zero probability of being selected
- The sample is not skewed toward internet-heavy, panel-savvy demographics
- Results can be projected to the full US adult population with statistical confidence

This is what separates MRI-Simmons from most other consumer intelligence tools, which rely on convenience samples or online panels that systematically over-represent certain demographics. The probabilistic approach is also why MRI-Simmons USA holds accreditation from the Media Rating Council (MRC) — one of only a handful of consumer datasets to do so.

## Survey Methodology: MRI-Simmons USA

In 2021, MRI-Simmons merged its two legacy studies — MRI's Survey of the American Consumer and Simmons' National Consumer Study (NCS/NHCS) — into a single unified dataset called **MRI-Simmons USA**.

**How the study works:**

- **Sample size**: 50,000+ US adults surveyed annually
- **Sampling approach**: Address-based probabilistic — respondents are randomly selected from address records, not self-recruited
- **Fielding**: Ongoing 12-month survey, structured as two 6-month waves per year
- **Release cadence**: Quarterly data releases (12 months of data per release)
- **Coverage**: All 50 states; includes 4,000+ English- and Spanish-speaking Hispanic respondents
- **Youth extension**: Separate questionnaires for children and teens in surveyed households

The quarterly release cadence is important for media planners: consumer behavior shifts between annual studies, and quarterly refreshes let teams catch evolving trends in media consumption, brand switching, and spending intent.

### Connect Study

The **Connect** study extends MRI-Simmons USA with passively collected digital and mobile media data. Connect links 60,000+ consumer elements to detailed reach, time spent, and activity metrics across 10+ traditional and digital platforms — including smartphone app usage, website behavior, and streaming activity — collected via a passive measurement app installed on panelists' devices. This gives planners a single-source view that bridges survey-based consumer profiles with observed media behavior.

## The Catalyst Platform

**Catalyst** is MRI-Simmons' self-service analytics and activation platform. It is the primary interface through which clients access the underlying data.

Catalyst is organized around four core modules:

### Profile
Define a target audience and generate a detailed psychographic and behavioral portrait. Cross-tabulation is the core analytical method — planners select a base population, define a target, and then analyze which attitudes, media habits, and brand behaviors over- or under-index in that target versus the general population.

### Analyze
Identify and measure key audience metrics, refine plans, and track performance over time. Includes interactive charts, custom formatting, and geo-mapping by census region, state, media market, or county.

### Activate
Push audience segments directly to programmatic platforms. This module is the bridge from planning to execution (see ACT below).

### Brand Catalyst
A predictive segmentation and brand strategy tool. Brand Catalyst uses advanced psychographics, purchase intent, and competitive positioning data to generate consumer personas and predict brand preference — without requiring the user to build manual cross-tabs.

### Platform Features

- **Boards**: A newer feature that lets users compile and share data visualizations from multiple Catalyst modules in a single shareable view
- **Interactive Quick Reports**: Templated data visualizations for common profiling and analytics tasks
- **Geo Mapping**: Audience display by region, state, DMA, or county
- **Shareable Dashboards**: Reports can be saved and distributed across teams
- **Export Options**: Flexible export formats for use in presentations and planning documents

The Catalyst platform is also the primary interface for **Simmons Insights**, the name used in academic and library licensing contexts for the same underlying platform.

## Audience Activation: ACT

**ACT (Activate Campaign Targets)** is the activation layer built into Catalyst, launched in March 2021. ACT closes the loop between consumer research and media execution.

The workflow:
1. **Build**: Define a target audience inside Catalyst using MRI-Simmons data
2. **Label**: Document and organize the audience definition for use across teams and campaigns
3. **Distribute**: Push the segment to virtually any DMP, DSP, SSP, CDP, or addressable media platform

The practical value of ACT is consistency. Without it, a campaign audience defined in a planning tool and an audience activated in a DSP are typically defined differently — by different criteria, different data sources, or different team members. ACT keeps the same audience definition intact from the moment it is created through the final media buy.

ACT also addresses a longstanding gap in media measurement: it allows planners to validate that the audience they bought is the audience they actually reached, since the same MRI-Simmons segments that inform the plan inform the activation.

In March 2026, MRI-Simmons and NIQ joined Comscore's Data Partner Network via Proximic, enabling MRI-Simmons consumer research to power contextual, ID-free targeting through leading programmatic platforms. This extends ACT's reach into privacy-first activation environments where identity-based targeting is unavailable.

## Pricing

MRI-Simmons pricing is **enterprise-only** with no publicly listed tiers or amounts. The platform is sold through a sales-led process with custom licensing for agencies, brands, media companies, and research teams. Third-party sites that list specific price ranges are speculative — actual pricing depends on the scope of data access, number of users, and modules licensed.

Academic and library access is available through institutional subscriptions (many university libraries provide student access to the Catalyst/Simmons Insights platform as part of their database licenses).

There is no free trial or self-serve signup for commercial users. Contact MRI-Simmons directly for a quote.

## MRI-Simmons vs Alternatives

### MRI-Simmons vs [GWI](/gwi)

GWI is a global consumer survey platform covering 53 markets and 2.8 billion consumers, with a modern SaaS pricing model and built-in AI tools. MRI-Simmons is US-only but covers the American consumer at a depth GWI does not match — 60,000+ elements versus GWI's broader but shallower global dataset.

Key differences:
- **Geography**: GWI covers 53+ markets globally; MRI-Simmons is US-only
- **Sample size (US)**: GWI USA collects 80,000 respondents; MRI-Simmons USA collects 50,000+, but uses probabilistic sampling vs. GWI's online panel
- **Pricing model**: GWI uses a per-user SaaS model with accessible tiers; MRI-Simmons is enterprise-only
- **AI tools**: GWI has built-in AI (Agent Spark, Canvas); MRI-Simmons does not have comparable AI features
- **Activation**: Both offer audience activation; MRI-Simmons' ACT integrates more natively with the planning workflow

Choose MRI-Simmons for deep US audience research and planning-to-activation workflows. Choose [GWI](/gwi) for global research, international brand strategy, or teams looking for more accessible pricing.

### MRI-Simmons vs [Scarborough](/scarborough)

[Scarborough](/scarborough) (owned by Nielsen) is built specifically for local market planning across 83+ US DMAs, with strong coverage of local broadcast and out-of-home media. MRI-Simmons is primarily a national research tool, with local studies available but secondary to its national offering.

Key differences:
- **Geographic scope**: Scarborough is DMA-specific; MRI-Simmons is national with local extensions
- **Local broadcast depth**: Scarborough is the standard for local TV and radio planning; MRI-Simmons is stronger for national psychographic profiling
- **Activation**: MRI-Simmons has ACT for programmatic activation; Scarborough is focused on planning rather than digital activation

Many agencies use both platforms simultaneously — Scarborough for local market buys, MRI-Simmons for national brand and audience strategy.

### MRI-Simmons vs [Comscore](/comscore)

[Comscore](/comscore) measures actual campaign delivery and provides programmatic targeting segments through Proximic by Comscore. MRI-Simmons is primarily a pre-campaign research and planning tool.

Key differences:
- **Primary use case**: Comscore measures what happened; MRI-Simmons informs what to do
- **Data type**: Comscore uses behavioral measurement (observed ad delivery, content consumption); MRI-Simmons uses survey-based consumer intelligence
- **Activation**: Proximic by Comscore offers contextual ID-free targeting; MRI-Simmons activates survey-defined audiences via ACT

Notably, as of March 2026, MRI-Simmons and NIQ joined Comscore's Data Partner Network, meaning the two platforms are increasingly complementary rather than competing.

### MRI-Simmons vs [Resonate](/resonate)

[Resonate](/resonate) uses AI-powered models to build profiles of 250 million US consumers with 15,000+ attributes, updated continuously from real-time behavioral signals. MRI-Simmons is survey-based with a 50,000-respondent probabilistic sample released quarterly.

Key differences:
- **Data source**: Resonate uses real-time behavioral modeling; MRI-Simmons uses probabilistic surveys
- **Scale**: Resonate covers 250M individual US profiles; MRI-Simmons projects from a 50,000-person sample
- **Update frequency**: Resonate updates continuously; MRI-Simmons refreshes quarterly
- **Credibility model**: MRI-Simmons has MRC accreditation and 60+ years of methodological history; Resonate offers more dynamic but less institutionally validated signals

Choose MRI-Simmons for strategic planning where survey-based representativeness matters. Choose [Resonate](/resonate) for real-time intent-based targeting.

### MRI-Simmons vs [Nielsen Media Impact](/nielsen-media-impact)

[Nielsen Media Impact](/nielsen-media-impact) is a cross-media simulation and reach-frequency planning tool that uses Nielsen audience data. MRI-Simmons is a consumer intelligence platform.

Key differences:
- **Primary function**: Nielsen Media Impact is a reach/frequency optimizer; MRI-Simmons is a consumer profiling and research platform
- **Data source**: Nielsen Media Impact uses panel-based media measurement; MRI-Simmons uses probabilistic consumer surveys
- **Use in workflow**: These tools are typically used together — MRI-Simmons to define and understand the target audience, Nielsen Media Impact to optimize media weight against it

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is MRI-Simmons?**
MRI-Simmons is a US consumer research and intelligence platform, formed in 2019 from the merger of MRI and Simmons Research. It provides 60,000+ data elements on the American consumer — demographics, psychographics, brand preferences, and media habits — used by agencies and brands for media planning, audience profiling, and programmatic activation. It is majority-owned by NIQ (NielsenIQ) and has been an industry standard for consumer research for over 60 years.

**What is MRI-Simmons data and how is it collected?**
MRI-Simmons data comes from its flagship study, MRI-Simmons USA — an ongoing survey of 50,000+ US adults per year using address-based probabilistic sampling. Respondents are randomly selected from physical address records (not recruited from an opt-in panel), giving the dataset population-level representativeness. Data covers demographics, 1,000+ psychographic attitudes, 6,500+ brands, 1,000+ product categories, and media consumption across traditional and digital platforms. The study is released quarterly.

**What is Simmons Insights?**
Simmons Insights is an earlier name for the Catalyst platform — MRI-Simmons' self-service analytics interface. The name is still used in academic and library licensing contexts (many university libraries subscribe to "MRI-Simmons Insights" or "Simmons Catalyst"). Commercially, the platform is called Catalyst. The underlying data is the same.

**How does MRI-Simmons differ from online panel surveys?**
MRI-Simmons uses address-based probabilistic sampling: every US adult has a known probability of being selected, regardless of whether they are active online or belong to a research panel. Online panels recruit self-selecting participants, which systematically over-represents certain demographics and can introduce unmeasurable bias. MRI-Simmons' approach is why its core study holds MRC accreditation — a credential that requires demonstrated methodological rigor and independent auditing.

**What is the Catalyst platform?**
Catalyst is MRI-Simmons' self-service analytics and activation platform. It provides four core modules: Profile (consumer cross-tabulation and profiling), Analyze (audience metrics and visualization), Activate (programmatic audience activation via ACT), and Brand Catalyst (predictive brand segmentation). Features include interactive charts, geo-mapping, shareable dashboards, and flexible data export.

**What is ACT (Activate Campaign Targets)?**
ACT is the audience activation module within Catalyst, launched in 2021. It allows planners to push audience segments built from MRI-Simmons data directly to any DMP, DSP, SSP, CDP, or addressable media platform. The practical benefit is consistency: the audience defined during planning is the same audience activated in the buy, eliminating the drift that occurs when planning and execution teams work from different data sources.

**How much does MRI-Simmons cost?**
MRI-Simmons does not publish pricing. It is an enterprise product with custom licensing based on the modules accessed, user count, and organizational type (agency, brand, media company). There is no public tier structure and no free trial for commercial users. Academic access is available through institutional library subscriptions. Contact MRI-Simmons sales for a quote.

**How does MRI-Simmons compare to GWI?**
MRI-Simmons covers the US consumer at much greater depth (60,000+ elements, probabilistic sampling, MRC-accredited); GWI covers 53+ global markets with a modern SaaS pricing model and built-in AI tools. For US-only planning depth and activation workflows, MRI-Simmons is stronger. For global research or more accessible pricing, [GWI](/gwi) is the better choice.
