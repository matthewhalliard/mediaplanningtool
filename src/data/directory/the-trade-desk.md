## Frequently Asked Questions

**How much does The Trade Desk cost?**
The Trade Desk does not publish standard pricing. Platform fees are negotiated and typically estimated at approximately 15–20% of media spend (covering platform access, data, and measurement). Large-spend advertisers can negotiate reductions through joint business plans. Contact The Trade Desk or a certified agency partner for specific pricing.

**Is The Trade Desk only for large advertisers?**
The Trade Desk primarily serves mid-market to enterprise advertisers. It requires a minimum level of media spend to make the platform economics work — smaller brands may find self-serve DSPs or managed services more appropriate. The platform is accessible through agency partners as well as direct relationships.

**What is Kokai?**
Kokai is The Trade Desk's AI platform, introduced as a major interface and intelligence overhaul. It analyzes 15+ million ad opportunities per second and drives real-time campaign optimization, audience targeting, and performance prediction across all channels on the platform.

**What is Unified ID 2.0 (UID2)?**
UID2 is The Trade Desk's open-source identity framework that replaces third-party cookies with a privacy-preserving, consent-based identifier. It enables audience targeting and measurement in a post-cookie environment while maintaining user control and compliance with privacy regulations.

**How does The Trade Desk compare to Google DV360?**
[Google DV360](/google-dv360) is Google's enterprise DSP. Unlike The Trade Desk, DV360 is tightly integrated with Google's walled garden (YouTube, Google Display Network), which some advertisers view as a conflict of interest. The Trade Desk is fully independent, providing objective optimization without preferencing any specific inventory source.

**How does The Trade Desk compare to Amazon DSP?**
[Amazon DSP](/amazon-dsp) offers unique access to Amazon's first-party shopping data and inventory. The Trade Desk covers a broader universe of the open internet with more publisher diversity and greater independence. Advertisers often run both platforms for complementary coverage.

**What channels does The Trade Desk support?**
The Trade Desk supports connected TV (CTV), linear TV (via partnerships), display, video, audio, native, and digital out-of-home (DOOH) — providing true omnichannel programmatic reach across the open internet.

## The Trade Desk vs Alternatives

### The Trade Desk vs Google DV360
[Google DV360](/google-dv360) is deeply integrated with Google's ecosystem — an advantage for YouTube and Google Display Network buyers, but a potential conflict of interest for advertisers seeking truly objective optimization. The Trade Desk's independence is its core differentiator. Teams with significant Google inventory may use both; teams prioritizing open internet diversity typically prefer The Trade Desk.

### The Trade Desk vs Amazon DSP
[Amazon DSP](/amazon-dsp) is the platform of choice for advertisers leveraging Amazon's retail first-party data for targeting. The Trade Desk covers a broader open internet footprint with more publisher choice and independent optimization. Many enterprise advertisers run both platforms to access complementary audiences.

### The Trade Desk vs Basis
[Basis](/basis) is an integrated media management platform with a built-in DSP component, designed to manage planning, buying, and analytics in one environment. The Trade Desk is a pure-play DSP with deeper programmatic optimization capabilities. Agencies that need a full workflow management platform alongside buying often use Basis; those prioritizing DSP performance opt for The Trade Desk.

### The Trade Desk vs Halliard
[Halliard](/halliard) is a media planning and performance management platform — not a buying platform. Teams typically use Halliard for cross-channel planning and performance visibility, and activate programmatic buys through The Trade Desk. The two operate at different layers of the media workflow and are complementary rather than competitive.

### The Trade Desk vs Criteo
[Criteo](/criteo) specializes in commerce media and retail media network access, with strong retargeting capabilities. The Trade Desk is a broader open internet DSP. Advertisers with significant e-commerce and retargeting goals often evaluate both; those focused on prospecting and brand reach tend to prioritize The Trade Desk.
